## What the operations teach

- A BGR pixel has three intensities. Grayscale approximates `0.114B + 0.587G +
  0.299R`; pure blue/green/red become about 29/150/76. This reduces `(H,W,3)`
  to `(H,W)` while keeping uint8 values from 0 to 255.
- Gaussian filtering averages a neighborhood with larger weights near its
  center. For our 3x3 case the weights are `[[1,2,1],[2,4,2],[1,2,1]] / 16`.
  A center impulse of 240 contributes 60 at the center and spreads to neighbors.
  This smooths fluctuations, but also smooths character edges.
- Median filtering sorts neighboring values and selects the middle one. For
  `[20,21,250]`, the median is 21. In the tested 3x3 example with four 10s, four
  20s and one 250, the median is 20. It rejects isolated spikes, but can round
  corners or remove very thin strokes. Our noisy document uses Gaussian noise,
  so the impulse example alone does not establish the best document filter.
- A histogram counts pixels at each intensity: `[0,0,128,255]` gives counts 2,
  1 and 1 at those three bins. A narrow range can indicate low contrast.
  Global equalization uses the cumulative histogram to redistribute intensities;
  it can stretch faint text and background noise together.
- CLAHE uses local tile histograms, clips large histogram counts and redistributes
  clipped counts, then interpolates between tiles. It limits local enhancement,
  but does not guarantee noise removal. Grid `(8,8)` means 8 columns by 8 rows
  of tiles, not tiles measuring 8 pixels.
- Contrast here is `sqrt(mean((gray - mean(gray))**2))`, evaluated in float64
  with population `ddof=0`. Units are grayscale intensity levels. Borders,
  background and noise affect it; a higher value does not imply better text/OCR.

## Functions and fixed experiment settings

`load_image` is preserved from SG01: BGR uint8, EXIF/alpha handling, at most 1800
pixels on the longest side. All eight images remain 1000x1400 in array height/width.

`to_grayscale(image_bgr)` validates nonempty BGR uint8 input and returns a new gray
array. `denoise_image(gray, method="median", kernel_size=3)` supports `none`,
`gaussian`, `median`; kernels must be positive odd integers (booleans rejected).
Kernel 1 and `none` return independent copies. Gaussian uses `sigmaX=0`, letting
OpenCV derive sigma from size, with `BORDER_REFLECT_101`. Median uses OpenCV's
replicated borders. Both experiments use kernel **3**.

`enhance_contrast(gray, method="clahe", clip_limit=2.0, tile_grid_size=(8,8))`
supports `none`, `equalize` and `clahe`. CLAHE uses **clipLimit=2.0**, grid **8x8**;
clip limit must be positive/finite and grid entries positive integers. These
arguments are checked even when a different contrast method is selected.
All results are independent uint8 arrays; input arrays are preserved.

`compare_methods` creates original, grayscale, Gaussian, median, equalized and
CLAHE alternatives. Each filter/enhancement starts directly from the same gray
image; competing methods are not chained. `run_experiments` checks input hashes,
writes full-size PNGs, figures, histogram counts, CSV/JSON metrics, versions and
settings. `main` exposes paths and settings through argparse. RGB conversion is
used only for plotting original color; gray figures use a fixed 0..255 range.
Full figures are 1800x960; detail figures 1800x480; histograms 1560x480.
The common detail crop is `(x0,y0,x1,y1)=(65,190,1100,420)`, within the fields area.
Histograms have 256 bins and a labeled log pixel-count axis.

## Visual inspection and provisional recommendations

Assistant inspected clear income, low-contrast income and noisy caste full-page
comparison figures, their corrected detail figures, and low-contrast/noisy
histograms. This is not a human student review or an inspection of all eight
comparison sets. All eight were generated and checked numerically/for decoding.

- Clear income: grayscale preserves visible text; Gaussian softens letter edges;
  median rounds some small corners. Global equalization makes text darker and
  heavier. No field line disappears in the inspected outputs. Keep an unfiltered
  original/grayscale alternative for clear documents.
- Low-contrast income: gray stddev 9.3507; equalized 55.7899 with visibly darker
  text, CLAHE 10.8864 with a milder change at clip 2.0/grid 8x8. Equalization is
  a candidate for this uniformly faint sample; this is not an OCR conclusion.
- Noisy caste: Gaussian and median reduce background speckle; Gaussian visibly
  blurs character edges. Global equalization increases speckle strongly while
  raising stddev from 42.4010 to 97.9022. CLAHE also makes speckle stronger, though
  less than equalization. Avoid blindly enhancing this noisy image; compare a
  small denoise candidate first in later pipeline/OCR experiments.
